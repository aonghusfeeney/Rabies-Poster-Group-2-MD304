Where to go next on rabies and the elimination target — and the ten primary sources this poster was built on.

Companion to the e-poster 100% Preventable, 100% Fatal: Barriers to rabies elimination on the road to “Zero by 30” · School of Medicine, University of Galway

Links were checked in October 2026. Everything in the first three sections is freely accessible; some of the journal articles in the last section are not — see the note there.

Start here

If you read two things

  • WHO rabies fact sheet The quickest authoritative overview — transmission, prophylaxis, elimination status. Two pages. The right first stop if rabies is one topic among many for you.
  • Rabies prevention and control in the WHO African Region Weekly Epidemiological Record 2026;101(19):78-86. Where most of the access and financing figures on this poster come from, including the barrier rankings and the immunoglobulin availability data. Short, and more readable than its title suggests.

The elimination target

Zero by 30

  • Zero by 30: the global strategic plan to end human deaths from dog-mediated rabies by 2030 The target itself — its objectives, its logic and its signatories. The document behind the phrase everyone quotes. Also hosted by WOAH.
  • Global Alliance for Rabies Control Country profiles, the stepwise elimination tool, and the clearest plain-language account of how an elimination programme actually runs on the ground. Useful if the policy documents feel abstract.

Ireland

Surveillance, guidance and what to do after an exposure

  • HPSC — rabies The Health Protection Surveillance Centre's rabies hub: Irish surveillance, risk classification and guidance for clinicians.
  • HPSC — post-exposure assessment and treatment guidelines PDF. The operational pathway used in Ireland: exposure categories, what to give, when, and to whom.
  • HPSC — rabies epidemiological data Irish case data and rabies-free status.
  • HSE immunisation — rabies The Irish vaccine schedule position, plus the NIAC 2025 updated recommendations on rabies vaccines.
